1. What is the difference between evidence and scientific evidence?
	All scientific evidence is evidence, but not all evidence is scientific. Scientific evidence requires a strict methodology.
2. How is evidence accumulated in scientific research?
	Evidence in scientific research is accumulated in two main ways:
	- through **observations of phenomena** that occur naturally in the real world;
	- through **experiments** carried out in a laboratory or in other controlled conditions.
	The collected data is then interpreted according to the scientific method, and it usually goes towards supporting or rejecting a hypothesis.
3. What is a claim? How to prove a claim?
	A **claim** is a contentious or debatable assertion made by someone. The **burden of proof** always lies on the person who makes the claim.
	To prove a claim in science, the presenter must:
	- write a paper presenting their specific **findings**;
	- defend their **thesis** against all challenges before a **panel of judges** (i.e. peer reviewers or a scientific committee);
	- provide sufficient evidence and arguments to support their position.
