1. What is the aim of this research?
	The primary aim of the research is to analyze the life goals of the Russian population to understand the society's true ideological structure. By studying these goals, the researchers aim to evaluate the extent of long-term planning among citizens and predetermine the future social trajectory of the country’s development.
2. What problem is studied in the paper?
	The paper studies the problem of the lack of long-term life goals among a significant portion of Russian society. The researchers highlight a "high degree of detachment from long-term goal-setting," noting that nearly half of the population lives day-to-day. This lack of socially meaningful goals leads to the underuse of the society's "resource of modernization" and reflects unresolved social and domestic problems.
3. Which data/evidence is used for the purposes of this research?
	The research uses primary empirical data collected in 2017 through a sociological survey. The evidence includes demographic statistics of the respondents (gender, age, education level, geographical location, and average per capita income) as well as self-reported qualitative and quantitative data regarding their specific life goals, the timeframes for achieving them, and the nature of these goals (utilitarian, mixed, or idealistic).
4. Is the sample representative for the study?
	From the text's perspective: Yes, the authors explicitly state that the information was collected based on a "representative sample" of 700 people. They provide demographic breakdowns to prove this balance: 53% women and 47% men, an average age of 46, varied education levels (34% with advanced degrees), and wide geographical coverage (48% from large cities and regional capitals).
5. Critical academic note: While the authors claim it is representative, a sample of 700 people is relatively small for a country as vast and diverse as Russia. However, in sociological polling, if the sample is correctly stratified (which the authors imply by showing the balanced demographics), 700 respondents can be statistically sufficient to identify general trends.
6. What methodology is used?
	The methodology used is a quantitative and qualitative sociological survey (questionnaire/polling). The researchers collected empirical data from the 700 respondents and then applied classification and categorization methods. They divided the respondents' self-reported goals into three distinct categories (purely utilitarian, mixed, and purely idealistic) based on the subjects' own views and intentions.
7. What are the findings of the researchers?
	The key findings of the study include:
	1. **Lack of long-term goals:** 48% of Russians live day-to-day without future planning. Only 52.4% have a specific life goal.
	2. **Reasons for having no goals:** 25.9% of people simply reproduce their current everyday lifestyle, while 21.7% (mostly older people, averaging 67 years old) feel they have already achieved everything they can.
	3. **Timeframes:** The average time frame for achieving goals is 5 years (ranging from 3 years for travel/purchases to 8-9 years for paying off loans or children's futures).
	4. **Goal categories:** Goals are divided into utilitarian (material), idealistic (social-demographic or creative), and mixed. The "mixed" category is the most popular.
	5. **Correlation with status:** There is a positive correlation between a person's education/income level and the likelihood of having a life goal.
8. How can the results of the study be used in practice?
	In practice, these results can be used by policymakers, government officials, and sociologists to design targeted social and economic policies. Understanding that a large portion of society is excluded from the goal-setting process highlights the need for state programs that stimulate modernization, encourage professional self-realization, and help create broader societal values beyond mere utilitarian survival. It serves as a warning that without reversing this trend, the country will struggle to resolve its cultural and economic problems.
9. What are the limitations of this research?
	1. **Sample size:** Although called "representative," 700 respondents is a rather small sample to make absolute generalizations about the entire Russian population (over 140 million people).
	2. **Subjectivity of self-reporting:** The categorization of goals relies heavily on the respondents' self-reported views ("based on the views expressed by the respondents themselves"). People might not always accurately understand or truthfully report their deep-seated psychological motivations.
	3. **Time-bound data:** The data was collected in 2017. Socio-economic conditions, crises, and global events can drastically change people's life goals, meaning these findings might not fully reflect the current reality today.
